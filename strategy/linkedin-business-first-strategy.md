# Praxis LinkedIn Strategy — Business First, Tools Second

## Positioning

Praxis should not lead LinkedIn with AI hype. Lead with business pressure owners recognize, then show how Praxis thinks through the problem and chooses the right tool only after the job is clear.

Core line:

> Business first. Tools second. Implementation together.

## Audience

Owner-led companies, founders, operators, and growth-minded leaders who feel friction in follow-up, intake, workflows, accountability, or owner dependency.

## Content pillars

### 1. Problems businesses keep working around

Posts that name recurring friction:

- owner bottlenecks;
- follow-up gaps;
- messy intake;
- manual workflows;
- growth friction;
- tool confusion.

Format:

- "If every exception routes to the owner, you do not have a people problem first. You have a system problem."
- Explain the pattern.
- Explain what Praxis would map first.
- End with a simple question.

### 2. Business first, tools second

Posts that de-hype AI without sounding anti-tool.

Angles:

- AI is useful when the job is clear.
- Software cannot fix an undefined handoff.
- A chatbot is not a strategy.
- The tool is not the strategy. The judgment is.

### 3. The owner walk

Show the implementation philosophy:

- diagnosis;
- tool fit;
- side-by-side rollout;
- adjustment after contact with the real business.

### 4. Field notes / mini diagnostics

Short breakdowns of fictionalized or anonymized business situations:

- "A company says they need more leads. The real issue is response time."
- "A team says they need AI. The real issue is no one owns the next step."
- "A founder says staff are not accountable. The real issue is unclear decision rights."

### 5. Tool map education

Explain when to use different layers:

- Bitta for education/presentation/voice walkthrough;
- Fin or inbound systems for capture/notification/routing;
- CRM/workflow automation for handoff discipline;
- human review where judgment matters.

## Weekly cadence

- Monday: pressure-point post.
- Tuesday/Wednesday: field note or mini diagnostic.
- Thursday: tool-fit/AI judgment post.
- Friday: founder/operator reflection or implementation lesson.

## Post CTA style

Avoid hard selling. Use diagnostic CTAs:

- "What part of your business keeps getting worked around?"
- "If this sounds familiar, start by mapping the handoff before buying another tool."
- "Praxis starts with the problem, then chooses the tool."

## Avoid

- "AI will transform your business";
- generic productivity hacks;
- robotic AI graphics;
- claims that Bitta or any single tool owns the whole funnel;
- jargon before pain.

## First 10 post prompts

1. Your business does not need another AI demo. It needs the right next move.
2. If the owner is still the escalation point for everything, the bottleneck is designed into the system.
3. Follow-up gaps are rarely motivation problems. They are ownership and handoff problems.
4. The tool is not the strategy. The judgment is.
5. Before choosing AI, ask what job the system actually needs to do.
6. Messy intake creates messy delivery.
7. Growth does not create chaos. It reveals where the old system was held together by people.
8. ChatGPT waits for you to know what to ask. Praxis starts by helping name the problem.
9. Bitta, Fin, CRM, automation, custom build — the right layer depends on the job.
10. Implementation does not end at recommendation. The fix has to survive contact with the business.
